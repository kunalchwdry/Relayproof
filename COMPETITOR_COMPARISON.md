# RelayProof — competitor watch & comparison

**Research checked:** 10 October 2026 (Asia/Kolkata)  
**App reviewed:** `/src/App.jsx`, `src/data.js`, and the Verify/Issue/Archive components in this project.  
**Scope:** Public product pages, technical write-ups, and repositories. This is not a complete market census or an official Metropolis entrant list.

## Executive readout

**The current RelayProof build is a polished product demo, not yet a functioning provenance competitor.** Existing solutions already combine audio watermarks, C2PA credentials, certificate or identity records, and verification. Some offer real audio tooling today; RelayProof currently simulates those steps.

The only plausible remaining opening is a **specific, testable deployment gap**: a recipient-first check of an AI-voiced public-information/newsroom message after the exact OGG/Opus transformation used by its sharing channel, with a status/evidence trail that publishers and recipients actually need. AIWatermark already advertises OGG support; ContentLens claims browser-side audio soft-binding recovery; Digimarc documents C2PA and selected watermark recovery for MP3/WAV. The C2PA Soft Binding Resolution model already supports discovery across manifest repositories, so a multi-issuer registry, browser UI, blockchain, or “audio provenance” label is not novel on its own. This remaining wedge is a hypothesis, not an established market gap.

**Recommendation:** Continue only if we can demonstrate an actual, measured voice-note path and validate a workflow or trust/status need not already met by these products and C2PA’s resolver ecosystem. Do not pitch the current prototype as verifying audio or as more technically capable than the products below.

## 1. What the app actually does today

The app currently provides:

- Audio file selection/drop, in-browser playback, and a real local SHA-256 digest.
- A publisher declaration form that creates a **browser-local demo receipt** and adds it to the session archive.
- A receipt dialog/JSON export and four selectable **simulated** result states: matched, no record, uncertain, and mismatch.
- Explicit labels that C2PA, watermark, and Monad results are simulated or absent.

It does **not** currently:

- Detect AudioSeal, SynthID, or any other watermark.
- Read or cryptographically verify a C2PA manifest or certificate.
- Compare the uploaded audio with the sample receipt, or test a re-encoded derivative.
- Sign a real publisher credential, resolve a registry entry, revoke a record, or call Monad.

The result-scenario selector is independent of the uploaded file. In particular, choosing or uploading a file does not run the “matched” scenario against that file. The current “matched” card is a fictional fixture. Its simulated labels help, but before showing the app to judges or external users, keep the fixture visually separate from live local file inspection so nobody can mistake a sample result for a real verification.

**Developer components and research references, not full competing products:** [`audiofp`](https://github.com/themankindproject/audiofp) describes classical audio fingerprints, in-memory matching, streaming extraction, and AudioSeal-compatible ONNX detection. Its documented decoder table names OGG-Vorbis, so Opus-in-Ogg support should be tested before choosing it. The open-licence [`soft-bindings-benchmark`](https://github.com/Original-Pictures/soft-bindings-benchmark) provides code/results and a preprint benchmarking watermark/fingerprint methods for C2PA soft bindings across images, audio, and video. Its method explicitly calibrates false-match rates on held-out negatives and estimates uncertainty by source item. It is a useful evaluation reference, not a recipient product or issuer registry; the committed results have not been independently reproduced here.

**Adjacent UX and research leads, not equivalent full products:** [`Provenance Lens`](https://github.com/Blimp3/provenance-lens-extension) is a user-triggered browser extension for image C2PA checks and supported OpenAI provenance signals in images/short audio; it is not a general-purpose audio watermark verifier. [`EM-Audio`](https://github.com/1D-Coda/em-audio) is a research/reference implementation for sample-exact provenance of derived audio and signed transport, rather than a publisher-facing origin/identity service. [`sealedrecord`](https://github.com/juanlentino/sealedrecord) verifies a signed, hash-chained session log and exact PCM/WAV anchors, but documents no audio analysis, C2PA, compressed-audio anchor, or record-creation UI; it is adjacent evidence-log prior art, not an AI-audio provenance competitor. [`Tracefern`](https://github.com/provemark/tracefern-image-check) is described by its repository as cross-media, but the README reviewed documents a WordPress C2PA verifier for JPEG/PNG/WebP images only; treat audio support as unverified until the implementation/docs agree.

**Technical implication:** A watermark is not identity by itself. A recovered payload or “watermark present” result needs to lead to a signed record, then be checked against an independent content match and issuer status. A C2PA manifest is useful when present, but a real OGG/Opus recovery/sidecar path must be tested; do not assume every C2PA tool embeds or resolves manifests in OGG.

## 5. Competitive conclusion

- **The current implementation loses on functional maturity.** Its strongest present assets are visual polish, clear trust-boundary copy, and a coherent publisher-to-recipient story—not provenance verification.
- **The broad concept is not novel.** Watermark + C2PA + manifest resolution is already demonstrated or marketed by AIWatermark, Resemble, and AWS; ContentLens and Digimarc offer browser recipient-side validation/recovery for selected audio credentials; C2PA defines SBR-based manifest discovery. Real local verification/issuer binding also exists in tools such as provcheck. RedoSan is a broad client-side authenticity suite with WAV watermark algorithms and DID tools, even though its inspected C2PA support is documented for images. Blockchain-backed media provenance is also active in the same hackathon track.
- **The defensible wedge is narrower:** a simple, mobile recipient experience for an AI-voiced local-news/public-information message, with an actual tested Opus derivative and a portable issuer/status history. This is a hypothesis to validate, not a claim of uncontested market space.
- **Monad is a supporting infrastructure choice, not the product differentiator.** If there is only one publisher, a signed database may be simpler. Show why multiple independent issuers need a shared, auditable registry.

## 6. Priority changes before the next demo

1. **Separate fixture mode from real-file mode.** Selecting an audio file should show only what was actually computed. Keep sample outcomes behind an unmistakable “Demo scenario” control.
2. **Run the feasibility test before adding more UI.** Use synthetic speech plus unmarked/human comparison clips; transcode to several recorded OGG/Opus settings; test short excerpts; collect detection rates, false positives, false negatives, and content-match failures. AIWatermark’s provider-reported music results do not predict speech performance.
3. **Implement one real verification path end-to-end:** credential signature and certificate check → soft-binding lookup → secondary content match → issuer/status lookup → careful result. Use “uncertain” on conflicts; do not treat a short watermark payload as proof of identity.
4. **Only then add Monad.** Anchor issuer/certificate fingerprints, credential digests, and revocation/supersession events; keep audio and private editorial data off-chain. Show real testnet transaction and resolver evidence.
5. **Validate the user workflow.** Ask a newsroom or public-information publisher whether it would issue such records and whether recipients would check them. If not, re-scope before investing further.

**Go/no-go:** If the chosen watermark/soft-binding method cannot recover a valid, content-matched record through the exact voice-note transformation, do not claim “survives re-encoding.” Narrow the supported cases or pivot the demo to a clearly labelled robustness test bench.
